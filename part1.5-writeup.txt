Part 1.5 compression across morphological types

part1.5-analysis.py trains both tokenizers on the concatenated cleaned training text of all seven languages, the same corpus as the BPE run. BPE is trained once to 50,000 merges; a smaller vocabulary is the prefix of that merge list, which is the same model training would have stopped at. Unigram is trained separately at each size with the same SentencePiece settings as part 1.4. The compression ratio is tokens divided by Unicode characters on each language's cleaned dev.txt. Lower means one token covers more of the text. The curves are in artifacts/part1/analysis/compression.svg, and the numbers are in compression_ratios.tsv.

BPE at 50,000, tokens per character: Finnish 0.260, German 0.268, Vietnamese 0.270, Hungarian 0.280, Russian 0.288, Arabic 0.297, Mandarin 0.637. The same order, with Mandarin far above the others, is already there at 5,000 (Finnish 0.421, Mandarin 0.988) and the lines fall smoothly as the vocabulary grows. From 5,000 to 50,000 every language sheds about 35 to 40 percent of its tokens. Typology changes the height of the curve more than the slope.

Mandarin is the outlier under both tokenizers. At 50,000 BPE a token covers about 1.6 characters; at 5,000 it covers about 1.0. Mandarin is isolating, and the script already writes roughly one morpheme per character, so a larger vocabulary mostly buys frequent two-character words. There is no long stem-plus-suffix chain for a subword piece to store.

Vietnamese is isolating too, and it does not behave like Mandarin. At 50,000 BPE it sits with German and Finnish (0.270). Vietnamese words do not inflect much, but they are written in a Latin alphabet as syllables of several letters. The tokenizer compresses those letter sequences. Isolating morphology limits the affix inventory; it does not by itself pin the ratio near one token per character. The Mandarin result is the logographic script together with the morphology.

Finnish, the clearest agglutinative case, is the best compressed language at every BPE size. A typical word is a stem plus a chain of case, possessive, and clitic suffixes, and those suffixes recur across the corpus, so a merge can store one and reuse it. One token then covers several characters of word-internal structure. Hungarian is agglutinative as well and stays in the same alphabetic band, but it is a little worse than Finnish at every size (0.460 to 0.280, against Finnish 0.421 to 0.260). Agglutination helps. It does not separate Hungarian from German by a wide gap.

The fusional languages are not one group. German (0.268 at 50,000 BPE) compresses almost as well as Finnish. German inflection is fusional, but compounding writes long words as a concatenation of stems, and that concatenative structure is what byte-pair merges latch onto. Russian (0.288) and Arabic (0.297) compress less. Russian endings are short and pack several features into one suffix, so there is less repeated internal material than in Finnish. Arabic word formation is mostly non-concatenative: a root and a pattern interleave, so a contiguous piece is a weaker match to a morpheme. Both are still far below Mandarin, because the writing system is alphabetic and letter sequences remain shareable.

Unigram tells the same linguistic story only once the vocabulary is large enough to hold the alphabet. The joint corpus has about 11,400 characters. Byte fallback needs 256 pieces plus an unknown token, so a vocabulary of 5,000 or 10,000 can keep the frequent characters and only a few hundred multi-character pieces. Those two models are nearly character tokenizers: Finnish stays near 0.66 tokens per character, and Mandarin near 0.97. The lines are almost flat there, including a tiny rise for German (0.644 to 0.647) and Russian (0.795 to 0.806), because the extra slots between 5,000 and 10,000 go to rare characters rather than to longer pieces. At 20,000 the full character inventory fits and several thousand slots become subwords, and the alphabetic languages drop at once (Finnish 0.662 to 0.364). At 50,000, unigram is close to BPE and still a bit worse on every language (Finnish 0.273 against 0.260, Mandarin 0.681 against 0.637).

BPE does not have that flat stretch. Its base vocabulary is the 256 bytes, so each new merge is a multi-byte piece from the first vocabulary size onward. That is why BPE is ahead at 5,000 and 10,000, and why its curves slope the whole way.

The comparison that the plot supports: languages whose orthography concatenates reusable parts, whether agglutinative suffixes (Finnish, Hungarian) or compound stems and syllable spellings (German, Vietnamese), compress to well under 0.3 tokens per character at 50,000. Fusional and non-concatenative morphology (Russian, Arabic) land a little higher. Mandarin, where the character is already the morpheme, stays near 0.64 for BPE and 0.68 for unigram. A larger vocabulary helps all seven; it does not close the gap that the writing system and the word-internal structure open.
